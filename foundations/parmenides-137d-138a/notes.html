Round & Straight — Critical Notes¶

Literal Greek Mathematical Philology & Reception · Parmenides 137d–138a

I. Exact Geometric & Symbolic Reconstruction¶

  1. Equidistant Circle & Optical Straight Line (*Parmenides* 137e, 145b):

    • Circle (O, C) centered at O \(= (0,0)\) exhibits equal radial quadrances \(Q(O, A) = Q(O, C) = Q(O, B) = 1\) (‘that whose extremes are everywhere equally distant from the middle’, 137e1–3, verbatim ancestor of Euclid Elements I, Def. 15).

    • Collinear diameter A O C exhibits Plato’s optical straightness (epiprosthēsis, 137e3–4): looking from A toward C, midpoint O blocks (ἐπίπροσθεν) both extremes—bridging Euclidean geometry and Euclidean Optics!

II. Literal Greek Mathematical Philology (Contra Paraphrasim)¶

  • Parmenides 137e3–4 — οὗ ἂν τὸ μέσον ἀμφοῖν τοῖν ἐσχάτοιν ἐπίπροσθεν ᾖ: Jowett translates ‘that of which the middle intercepts the view of the extremes’. As Heath (Euclid Vol. I, pp. 165–166; plato:parm:137E) and Proclus (Friedlein 109.21; plato:parmenides:137e) observe, Euclid deliberately replaced Plato’s optical ἐπίπροσθεν (which appeals to visual rays) with the purely intrinsic geometric definition ἥτις ἐξ ἴσου τοῖς ἐφ’ ἑαυτῆς σημείοις κεῖται (‘a line which lies evenly with the points on itself’, I, Def. 4), while retaining the optical principle as Postulate 1 of Euclid’s Optics!
