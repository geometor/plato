Incommensurability — Synopsis¶

The Three Mathematical Disciplines & Dimensional Incommensurability · Laws VII, 817e–820d

Architectural Thesis¶

Unit VI gathers the late-Platonic axiomatic, ontological, aesthetic, and pedagogical foundations of geometry across Parmenides, Philebus, and Laws Book VII:

  1. Dimensional Incommensurability (*Laws* VII 817e–820d): In his final work, the Athenian Stranger mandates three liberal mathematical disciplines (arithmetic, metrics of line/surface/solid, and celestial kinematics) and castigates Greek education for assuming that length (O C, \(Q=1\)), plane surface (O D, \(Q=2\)), and solid depth (O E, \(Q=3\)) are universally commensurable.
