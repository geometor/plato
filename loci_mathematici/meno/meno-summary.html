Unit I — Meno: Definition of Figure, Doubling the Square & Geometric Hypothesis — Structural Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Interactive Diagram Guide · Meno 75d–76a, 81e–85d, 86e–87b

Architectural Thesis¶

Unit I gathers the three foundational mathematical episodes of Plato's Meno—the earliest surviving Greek text to stage an explicit geometric diagram step by step:

  1. Dialectical Definition of Figure (75d–76a): Before defining virtue, Socrates offers Meno a model geometric definition: roundness (strongylotēs) is merely a figure, whereas universal figure (schēma) is the limit of solid (στερεοῦ πέρας, 76a).

  2. Anamnesis & Doubling the Square by the Diagonal (81e–85d): Answering Meno’s eristic paradox of inquiry, Socrates summons an untutored household attendant and constructs the \(2 \times 2\) square ABCD (\(Q = 4\)). After exposing two false arithmetic intuitions—doubling the side to AE (\(4 \times 4 = 16\), AEFG) and taking the sesquialter side AM (\(3 \times 3 = 9\), AMNP)—Socrates draws the four diagonals BD, BH, HK, and KD, cutting each \(4\)-foot square in half (\(2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8\)) to enclose the exact double square BHKD.

  3. Inquiry from Hypothesis & Application of Area (86e–87b): Pressed to ask whether virtue is teachable prior to knowing its essence, Socrates adopts the geometer’s method of hypothesis (ἐξ ὑποθέσεως, 86e): reducing the inscription of a triangular area inside a given circle to an elliptic application of area along the diameter.
