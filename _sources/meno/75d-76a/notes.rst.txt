===========================================
Definition of Figure — Critical Notes
===========================================

.. rubric:: Literal Greek Mathematical Philology & Reception · Meno 75d–76a

I. Geometric Significance
-------------------------

In *Meno* 75d–76a, Socrates introduces the canonical philosophical definition of :term:`figure <schema>` (*schēma*): that in which a solid terminates (*στερεοῦ πέρας*). This passage provides the philosophical foundation for:

- :cite:`Euclid Elements I, Def. 14 <euclid:I.def.13>`: *"A figure is that which is contained by any boundary or boundaries."*
- :cite:`Euclid Elements XI, Def. 2 <euclid:I.def.13>`: *"The extremity of a solid is a surface."*

II. Literal Greek Mathematical Philology (*Contra Paraphrasim*)
---------------------------------------------------------------

- **76a7 — στερεοῦ πέρας σχῆμα εἶναι**: :person:`Jowett <jowett>` translates *'figure is the limit of solid'*, faithfully preserving :person:`Burnet's <burnet>` technical pairing of :term:`stereon <stereon>` and :term:`peras <peras>`. Compare :cite:`Elements XI, Def. 2 <euclid:I.def.13>` (*στερεοῦ δὲ πέρας ἐπιφάνεια*, 'the extremity of a solid is a surface') and :cite:`I, Def. 14 <euclid:I.def.13>` ('a figure is that which is contained by any boundary or boundaries').

III. Canon Reception
--------------------

- **Aristotle** (:cite:`Anal. Post. I.1, 71a29 <aristotle:anal-post>`; ``plato:meno``): Cites the Meno inquiry to illustrate how demonstrative science requires pre-existent nominal understanding of figure and boundary.
- **Proclus** (:cite:`In Euclidem, Friedlein 136–143 <proclus:in-euclidem>`; ``plato:meno:76A``): Devotes extended commentary to Plato's definition of figure as limit of solid, reconciling it with Euclid's definitions.
