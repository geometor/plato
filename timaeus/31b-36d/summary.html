World-Soul & Means — Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Interactive Diagram Guide · Timaeus 31b–36d

Architectural Thesis¶

In Timaeus 31b–36d, Timaeus of Locri presents the grandest proportional and harmonic synthesis of antiquity, constructing both the four-element body of the cosmos and the musical fabric of the World-Soul from exact rational and quadratic invariants:

  1. Single vs. Double Geometric Means (31b–32c): To bind fire and earth together, the Demiurge employs continuous geometric proportion (ἀναλογία), the fairest of bonds. Had the cosmos been a two-dimensional plane without depth, one mean proportional would have sufficed (\(a^2 : ab : b^2\), Elements VIII.11); because the cosmos is a three-dimensional solid, two mean proportionals are required (\(a^3 : a^2 b : a b^2 : b^3\), Elements VIII.12), placing air and water between fire and earth so that fire : air = air : water = water : earth.

  2. Harmonic Division of the World-Soul (35a–36b): Slicing the mixed psychic substance into the geometric tetractys of doubles 1, 2, 4, 8 and triples 1, 3, 9, 27 (spanning four octaves plus a major sixth, \(27:1\)), the Demiurge inserts into every interval the harmonic mean \(H = \frac{2ab}{a+b}\) (G H) and arithmetic mean \(A = \frac{a+b}{2}\) (O G), generating sesquialter fifths (\(3:2\)), sesquitertian fourths (\(4:3\)), and epogdoic whole tones (\(9:8\)), and filling every \(4:3\) fourth with two \(9:8\) tones plus the exact diatonic leimma 256 : 243.

  3. Circles of the Same and the Different (36b–d): Splitting the calibrated harmonic strip lengthwise and bending the two bands into great circles intersecting like the letter Chi (\(\mathrm{X}\), P Q across equator A B on (O, B)), the Demiurge establishes the celestial equator (Same) and the ecliptic subdivided in the seven ratios 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 27 (Different).
