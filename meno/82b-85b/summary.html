Slave-Boy Square Doubling — Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Interactive Diagram Guide · Meno 81e–85d

Architectural Thesis¶

Unit I gathers the three foundational mathematical episodes of Plato's Meno—the earliest surviving Greek text to stage an explicit geometric diagram step by step:

  1. Anamnesis & Doubling the Square by the Diagonal (81e–85d): Answering Meno’s eristic paradox of inquiry, Socrates summons an untutored household attendant and constructs the \(2 \times 2\) square ABCD (\(Q = 4\)). After exposing two false arithmetic intuitions—doubling the side to AE (\(4 \times 4 = 16\), AEFG) and taking the sesquialter side AM (\(3 \times 3 = 9\), AMNP)—Socrates draws the four diagonals BD, BH, HK, and KD, cutting each \(4\)-foot square in half (\(2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8\)) to enclose the exact double square BHKD.
