Slave-Boy Square Doubling — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

Exact Symbolic Reconstruction, Greek Mathematical Philology & Canon Reception · Meno 81e–85d

I. Exact Geometric & Symbolic Reconstruction¶

In geometor.plato.constructions.construct_meno_diagonal_doubling (see Demos), Socrates’ sand diagram (82b–85b) is modeled with 100% exact rational coordinates over \(\mathbb{Q}\) without introducing irrational square roots until linear magnitude is requested—directly vindicating Rational Trigonometry’s primacy of quadrance:

  • Given Two-Foot Square ABCD (82c–d): Anchored at A \(= (0,0)\) and B \(= (2,0)\), every side has quadrance \(Q(A,B) = 4\), enclosing area \(4\) square feet.

  • First Refutation — Doubling the Side (82e–83c): Extending A B to E \(= (4,0)\) produces the fourfold square AEFG of quadrance \(Q(A,E) = 16\) (composed of four \(4\)-foot squares meeting at interior center C \(= (2,2)\)).

  • Second Refutation — The Three-Foot Trial (83d–e): Searching strictly between \(2\) and \(4\), the attendant proposes the arithmetic midpoint M \(= (3,0)\) (sesquialter \(3:2\)), which generates square AMNP of quadrance \(Q(A,M) = 9 \neq 8\), bringing on the purifying aporia (84a–c).

  • Constructive Resolution via the Four Diagonals (84d–85b): Drawing the four diagonals BD, BH, HK, and KD across the four sub-squares cuts each \(4\)-foot square into two equal isosceles right triangles of area \(2\). The inscribed tilted square BHKD therefore has exact area and side quadrance:

    \[Q(B, D) = Q(A, B) + Q(A, D) = 4 + 4 = 8 = 2 \, Q(A, B).\]

II. Literal Greek Mathematical Philology (Contra Paraphrasim)¶

  • 85b4 — διάμετρον ὄνομα, καλοῦσιν δὲ ταύτην οἱ σοφισταί: Here σοφισταί denotes technical mathematical experts/professors rather than eristic rhetoricians; diametros means the diagonal of the square (preserved throughout Euclid and Aristotle).

III. Reception in Aristotle, Euclid, Proclus & Hoenen¶

  • Aristotle (Anal. Post. I.1, 71a29; plato:meno): Resolves Meno’s dilemma by distinguishing universal pre-existent knowledge (that every triangle has 2R) from simultaneous recognition (ἅμα ἐπαγόμενος) that the drawn figure falls under the universal.

  • Proclus (In Euclidem, Friedlein 45.15–46.3; plato:meno:81e-85d): Cites Socrates’ questioning of the boy as proof that mathematical logoi reside substantially within the soul rather than being stamped from outside by sense-impressions.

  • Hoenen (De Noetica Geometriae, Cap. I & VII; plato:meno:82b-85b): Highlights 82b–85b as the locus classicus for intellectual vision in the singular phantasm: the boy counts neither decimal approximations of \(2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828\) nor syllogistic major premises, but beholds universal necessity (\(4 \times \frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 8\)) directly in the bisected grid.
