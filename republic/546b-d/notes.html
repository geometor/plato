The Nuptial Number — Critical Notes¶

Philology, Mathematical Interpretations & Reception · Republic VIII 546b–d

I. Mathematical Architecture¶

The Platonic Nuptial Number (arithmos geōmetrikos) in Republic VIII 546b–d is the most celebrated mathematical crux in the Platonic corpus:

  • Epitritos Pythmēn (4:3): The epitritos is the 4:3 ratio (the diatonic fourth), and pythmēn denotes the lowest terms of the proportion—the legs \(3\) and \(4\) of the canonical Pythagorean right triangle.

  • Pempadi Syzygeis: Paired with the hypotenuse \(5\), forming the fundamental right-angled triangle \((3, 4, 5)\).

  • Involution & Evolution: The sum of cubes of the sides \(3^3 + 4^3 + 5^3 = 27 + 64 + 125 = 216\) equals \(6^3\), corresponding to the 216-day minimum period of human gestation.

  • Rational vs. Irrational Diameters: The diameter of a square of side \(5\) has length \(\sqrt{50}\). Its nearest rational approximation (diametros rhētē) is \(7\) (since \(7^2 = 49 = 50 - 1\)). The square on the rational diameter is less by one (\(49 = 50 - 1\)), while that on the irrational diameter is less by two (\(48 = 50 - 2\)).

II. Reception¶

  • Aristotle (Politics V.12, 1316a1–39): Criticizes Plato’s theory of cyclical state revolutions based on the geometrical number, asking why the ideal constitution must decline into timocracy rather than its opposite.

  • James Adam (The Nuptial Number of Plato, Cambridge, 1891): Solved the philological riddle by demonstrating that the master period is \((3 \times 4 \times 5)^4 = 60^4 = 12,960,000\) days (\(36,000\) years of \(360\) days), which equals \(100\) times \((3600 + 4800 + 2700)\) in cyclic harmonics.
